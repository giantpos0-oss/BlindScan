# BlindScan user guide

BlindScan turns print into speech. It was made by a blind person for blind people. It is free, and it always will be.

Reading happens entirely on your phone, and works the same in airplane mode. The one time anything leaves the phone is when you use Ask a question with BlindScan's online AI: then the document you ask about goes to it to be answered. See Ask a question, below.

This guide covers the latest version of BlindScan. To see what changed in each version, open the release notes from the top of the Help tab.

## Finding your way around

There are four tabs along the bottom of the screen: Live mode, Scan document, Help, and Settings.

At the top left of every screen except Live mode is a Navigate up button. On a document it goes back to the camera. In a settings group it goes back to the list of settings. Everywhere else it goes to Live mode.

Once you've scanned something, an Ask a question button sits at the bottom right of every screen. See Ask a question, below.

The first time you open Live mode or Scan document, BlindScan asks to use the camera. Press Allow camera, then allow it in the window Android shows you.

## The Help tab

At the very top of the Help tab are two buttons:

- Open user guide in browser opens this guide in Chrome, or whichever web browser you use.
- Open release notes in browser opens the list of what's new and what changed in each version.

Below the buttons, the whole user guide is right there in the app, so you can read it even with no connection. Every section title is a heading, so you can jump from section to section with your screen reader's heading navigation.

The browser versions are always the newest. Opening them is done by your browser. BlindScan itself still has no internet access.

## Live mode

Point the camera at anything with words on it: a package, a sign, a screen, a letter. As soon as the text is steady in view, it is read out. Move to something new and the new text is read. The same text isn't repeated while it stays in view.

Live mode reads with Android text to speech unless you change it in Settings, Live mode.

The controls work like a DVR:

- Rewind goes back one sentence.
- Play and Pause starts and stops speech.
- Fast forward skips ahead one sentence.

Using any of them pauses live reading, so new text doesn't interrupt you while you go back over something. Press Resume live reading to carry on. Pause live reading stops new text being read while you keep the camera pointed somewhere.

Light on turns on the phone's flashlight. In dim places the light comes on by itself. You can turn that off in Settings, Camera and positioning.

If you read live mode with your screen reader instead, the DVR controls are hidden and your screen reader speaks each new piece of text as it appears.

## Scan document

Use this for letters, bills, menus, labels and pages of a book.

### Positioning the phone

Lay the page on a table. Hold the phone flat with the screen facing up, about a foot above the page, with the top of the phone pointing away from you.

BlindScan talks you into position, one movement at a time. After each prompt there's time to move before the next one. Follow the prompts until you hear "Ready. All the text is in view" and feel a double buzz.

### What the positioning prompts mean

- "Lift the phone up a little": the text runs off both sides, so move the phone higher above the page.
- "Bring the phone a little closer to the page": the text is small in the picture. Lower the phone toward the page.
- "Move the phone a little to the left" or "to the right": slide the phone sideways, keeping it the same height.
- "Move the phone a little away from you": slide it toward the far edge of the page, the top of the page.
- "Move the phone a little toward you": slide it toward your body, the bottom of the page.
- "Hold the phone flatter, screen facing up": the phone is tipped. Level it out over the page.
- "Hold still": the picture is blurry from movement. Keep the phone steady for a moment.
- "No text in view yet": point the camera at the page, holding it flat about a foot above it.
- "It's dim, light on": the light came on by itself.

### Taking the picture

When you hear Ready, press Scan document now, or press either volume button.

While the Scan document tab is showing the camera, either volume button takes the picture, even with TalkBack on. On every other screen the volume buttons work normally. You can turn this off in Settings, Camera and positioning.

If you'd rather not press anything, turn on Scan automatically when ready in Settings, Camera and positioning. BlindScan then takes the picture by itself after it has been ready for about a second.

You'll hear "Scanning. Hold still." and feel a short buzz when the picture has been taken.

To scan a page and go straight to asking about it, press Scan and ask a question instead. You get the same positioning prompts. With Scan automatically when ready on, it takes the picture by itself when the page is in view; otherwise it takes it as soon as you press the button. You'll hear "Reading the page for your question." The page isn't read aloud: Ask a question opens as soon as it's read.

### Check view

Check view gives a full report whenever you want one: roughly how many lines of text it can see, whether any text runs off an edge, whether the page's edges are visible, and the lighting.

### The light

Light on and Light off control the phone's flashlight. When it's dim, BlindScan turns the light on by itself. Once you press Light on or Light off yourself, BlindScan leaves the light alone.

### Shaky hands welcome

No stand needed. When you press Scan document now or a volume button, BlindScan waits for a steady moment (at most two and a half seconds) and takes the picture right then. If your hand moved while the picture was being taken, it quietly takes a second one and keeps the sharper.

If pressing the button tips the phone or knocks the page out of view, BlindScan tells you how to fix it and takes the picture once you've settled again.

Glossy paper can reflect the phone's light and wash out words. Whenever the light is on during a scan, BlindScan also takes a picture with the light off, quickly checks which picture shows more text, and reads that one.

### Several pages: batch scanning

Turn on the Batch scanning switch on the Scan document tab. The big button becomes Scan page 1, Scan page 2, and so on.

Scan each page in turn. You'll hear "Page 1 captured", "Page 2 captured" and so on, and each page is read in the background while you turn to the next one.

When you're done, press Read pages (it says how many, like Read 3 pages). The pages open as one document, with a heading for each page so you can jump between them. A page with no text on it is left out, and BlindScan tells you so.

To ask about the pages instead of hearing them read, press Ask about pages (it also says how many, like Ask about 3 pages). The pages become one document and Ask a question opens straight away. Questions cover every page, so you can ask things like "what's the total on the last page" or "which page has the due date".

### Adding a page to a document

From an open document, choose Add another page from Document actions. The camera comes back and the big button says Scan page 2 (or whichever page is next). The new page is read on its own, then added to the document.

### Reading pictures and PDFs already on your phone

Press Import image or PDF on the Scan document tab and choose one file or several. Pictures and scanned PDF files both work. Every page of a PDF is read, one after another, into one document.

You can also share a picture or PDF to BlindScan from another app, such as Files, Photos or your email, or choose Open with BlindScan. It opens on the Scan document tab and is read straight away.

### Starting fresh

Press the Scan document tab again, even while a document is showing, to clear everything you scanned and start fresh. You'll hear "Scan cleared. Ready for a new scan."

Open last document brings back your most recent scan.

## Reading your document

As soon as you scan, the document opens and the first lines are read out while the rest of the page is still being read.

You choose who reads it in Settings, Reading scanned documents:

- My screen reader: each part is spoken the moment it is recognised, the same moment it appears on screen. This is the starting setting.
- Android text to speech, with DVR controls: the document is read with Rewind, Play and Pause, and Fast forward buttons, plus Back to start. The controls work straight away, while the page is still being read.

Each paragraph is its own item on the screen, so you can move through the document a paragraph at a time. "End of document" marks the end once every page has been read. While a page is still being read, you'll find "Reading this page" at the end.

### Headings in your document

Headlines in big type, and titles printed right over their text (such as a year on a timeline or Mains on a menu), become headings. On the document screen they are real headings, so you can jump between them with your screen reader's heading navigation. When a document has several pages, each page starts with a Page heading too.

A big caption printed under a photo stays ordinary text, not a heading.

Bold text that is the same size as the text around it can't be told apart from ordinary text yet, so a small bold subheading may come out as an ordinary paragraph.

### Document actions

The Document actions button at the top right of the document offers:

- Read aloud (Pause reading while it's reading)
- Ask a question
- Save to device as
- Share text
- Copy text
- Add another page
- Share scan for testing
- New scan
- Discard

With TalkBack, every paragraph also has these as actions. Set your reading control to Actions, then swipe up or down to pick one and double tap. Or open the TalkBack menu and choose Actions.

### Saving a document

Save to device as asks which kind of file you want:

- Plain text (TXT)
- Word document (DOCX)
- PDF
- Web page (HTML)
- Markdown
- E-book (EPUB)

Then choose where to save it. The suggested name is "Scan" followed by the date and time, and you can change it.

Headings are kept as real headings in Word, PDF, web page, Markdown and e-book files. The document title is the main heading, then a heading for each page if there are several, then the headings found on the page.

Share text sends the text to any app, such as email or messages. Copy text puts it on the clipboard.

### Share scan for testing

Share scan for testing sends the original photos with a report of what was recognised, how sure the app was, and how long it took. It's for sending a tricky scan to the developer so the reading can be improved. Nothing is sent unless you choose this and pick where to send it.

## Ask a question

Ask anything about the document you scanned, like: what's the total, when is it due, who is this letter from, which dishes are vegetarian.

You can also ask about things the document is about, even if the answer isn't written on it, like what a late fee is or what a medicine on a label is for. Those answers start with "This isn't in the document, but:", so you always know which answers come from the page. Questions that have nothing to do with the document aren't answered: you hear "That question isn't about this document."

- Press Ask a question at the bottom right of any screen, or choose Ask a question from Document actions, or from TalkBack's Actions on any paragraph. It asks about the document that's showing, or else the one you scanned most recently.
- Documents with several pages work the same way: batch scans, added pages and imported PDFs. If pages are still being read, you'll hear "Still reading the pages. You can ask as soon as they're done."
- When you go back from Ask a question to the document, it isn't read aloud again from the start. Choose Read aloud if you want to hear it.
- Type your question in the Your question box and press Ask, or press Say your question and speak it.
- You'll hear "Looking." Then you hear the answer, followed by "The document says:" and the exact words from the page that back it up. Both are also shown on screen.
- If the photo shows something different from what BlindScan read, like a different amount, you also hear "Please check:" and what the photo shows compared with the scan.
- Ask follow-up questions, like "and when is it due?". It remembers your last couple of questions.
- You can ask your next question at any time, even while an answer is still coming. You'll hear "Next question saved", and it's answered as soon as the one before it is done.
- After a spoken question, BlindScan listens for your next question by itself, once the answer has had time to be read. Just speak, or cancel the listening to stop. You can turn this off in Settings, Ask a question.
- After a typed question, the Your question box is ready for the next one.
- Press Stop to stop the speech. Navigate up, or any tab, closes Ask a question.

### Every answer is checked

The AI must give the exact words from the document that support its answer. BlindScan checks that those words, and every number in the answer, really are in the scanned text. If they aren't, you hear "I couldn't find that in the document, so I won't guess", instead of something the AI may have made up. If the document doesn't hold the answer, you hear "The document doesn't say."

For a one-page document, BlindScan's online AI also looks at the photo of the page, to catch words the camera misread. If the scan says $84.17 but the photo shows $64.17, it answers from the photo and tells you: "Please check: The photo shows $64.17 where the scan says $84.17." For documents with several pages, only the text is checked, so a misread word can still get through. For anything important, like an amount due or a medicine dose, check another way too.

### Which AI answers

- BlindScan's online AI works on every phone with an internet connection. You don't need an account or a key, and it's free. It's the most accurate, and it's the one that checks the photo. The document's text, the photo for a one-page document, and your question are sent to it and to OpenAI to answer. BlindScan's service doesn't keep them.
- Google's built-in AI, Gemini Nano, answers on the phone, so nothing leaves it. Recent Pixel phones, Samsung Galaxy S phones and others have it, but not rooted phones or phones with an unlocked bootloader. It doesn't check the photo.
- To start with, BlindScan uses the built-in AI when it's ready, and the online AI otherwise. You can choose in Settings, Ask a question.

## How BlindScan reads a page

- Pages with two or more columns are read one column at a time, top to bottom, left to right.
- Headlines come first. Labels on pictures, captions and page numbers are read after the main text, so they don't interrupt it.
- A big decorative first letter is joined back onto its word.
- A title printed over a short piece of text, like each year on a timeline, is read just before that text.
- Lines are joined into flowing paragraphs. Words split by a hyphen at the end of a line are put back together.
- Receipts and lists keep one item per line.
- Upside-down and sideways pages are turned the right way round automatically.

### Unclear words

When a word comes out unclear, BlindScan compares what it saw with similar English words and fixes the word only when the evidence is clearly better. Numbers are never changed into other numbers, and words it read confidently, like names, are left alone. You can turn this off in Settings, Accuracy and speed.

## Settings

Settings are grouped. Choose a group to see its settings, then use Navigate up or Back to return to the list. Every setting is saved the moment you change it.

### Reading scanned documents

- Read scanned documents with: My screen reader (the starting setting), or Android text to speech, with DVR controls.

### Live mode

- Read live mode with: My screen reader, or Android text to speech, with DVR controls (the starting setting).
- Best accuracy in live mode (slower): uses the bigger, more accurate reading engine in live mode. Off to start with.

### Speech

- Speech engine for Android text to speech: Same as the phone (whatever is set in Android's Text-to-speech settings), or any speech engine installed on the phone, just for BlindScan. Press Hear a sample to try it. This is the voice BlindScan uses when it reads with Android text to speech; your screen reader keeps its own voice.
- Speech rate for Android text to speech: from half speed to three times normal speed, in steps of a tenth. This only affects Android text to speech, not your screen reader.

### Accuracy and speed

- Correct unclear words using an English dictionary. On to start with. Numbers are never changed.
- Experimental fast math engine (XNNPACK). Off to start with. It may make reading faster on some phones. If a scan ever fails with it on, it turns itself off.
- Batch scanning: the same switch as on the Scan document tab.

### Camera and positioning

All of these are on to start with, except Scan automatically when ready.

- Spoken positioning prompts.
- Vibrate when ready to scan.
- Volume buttons take the picture.
- Turn the light on automatically in dim places.
- Tilt prompts (for pages lying flat). Turn this off if you scan things that aren't lying flat, like a sign on a wall.
- Scan automatically when ready.

### Ask a question

- Answers come from: Automatic (the phone's built-in AI when it's ready, otherwise BlindScan's online AI), BlindScan's online AI, or Phone's built-in AI only.
- With Phone's built-in AI only, if Android hasn't fetched the built-in AI yet, Ask a question offers Get the built-in AI.
- After a spoken question, listen for the next one. On to start with.

### Problems

This group only appears after BlindScan has closed unexpectedly. Share crash report sends the details to the developer through any app you choose, such as email.

## Tips for the best results

- Flat pages read best. Smooth out folds if you can.
- For small print, hold the phone a little closer, wait for Ready, and hold still while it scans.
- Glossy paper can reflect the light. If a scan comes back patchy, try with the light off, or tilt the page slightly away from overhead lights.
- Good, even light helps more than anything else. A lamp beside the page is better than one straight above it.
- Upside-down and sideways pages are fine: the app turns them the right way round by itself.

## When something goes wrong

- "No text found": the page wasn't in view, or the picture was too blurry or dark. Press Check view, follow the prompts until you hear Ready, then scan again. In a document with several pages, only that page is dropped; position it and scan again.
- "Couldn't take the picture": press Scan document now again. If it keeps happening, close BlindScan fully and open it again.
- "Couldn't open that file": the file you chose isn't a picture or a PDF BlindScan can read.
- BlindScan closed in the middle of a scan: next time it opens it tells you, turns off the fast math engine if it was on, and puts Share crash report in Settings, Problems.
- The volume buttons change the volume instead of scanning: check Settings, Camera and positioning, Volume buttons take the picture. They only take pictures while the Scan document camera is showing.
- Some words are wrong or missing on a page: send it with Share scan for testing, so the reading can be improved.

## Installing and updating

BlindScan isn't in the Play Store. It's free and open source, and you download it straight from its project page:

https://github.com/giantpos0-oss/BlindScan/releases/latest/download/BlindScan.apk

The first time, Android asks you to allow your browser or file manager to install apps. To update, download and install the newest copy the same way. It installs over the old one and keeps your settings.

BlindScan works on Android 8 and newer.

## Privacy

BlindScan only uses the internet for Ask a question with BlindScan's online AI. When you ask, the text of that document, the photo of a one-page document, and your question are sent to BlindScan's AI service and to OpenAI to answer. BlindScan's service doesn't keep them. Nothing else is sent. Choose Phone's built-in AI only in Settings, Ask a question, to keep everything on the phone (on phones that have it). Pictures are kept only in the app's private storage and are tidied away when you clear a scan. Text, photos and reports leave your phone only when you choose Share, Save, or Share scan for testing yourself.
